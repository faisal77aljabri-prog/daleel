Essay Advisor

AI essay coaching that understands Saudi cultural context — and what Western admissions officers actually want to read.

1. Choose a Prompt

Prompt 1 — Identity

Background, identity, interest, or talent that defines who you are.

Prompt 2 — Challenge

An obstacle or failure you faced and what you learned from it.

Prompt 3 — Belief

A belief or idea you questioned — what prompted that thinking?

Prompt 4 — Problem

A problem you've solved or would like to solve — intellectual or otherwise.

Prompt 5 — Growth

An accomplishment or realization that sparked personal growth.

Prompt 6 — Captivation

A topic or concept that captivates you so much you lose track of time.

Prompt 7 — Free Choice

Share an essay on any topic of your choice.

🇸🇦 Saudi Story Angles

Themes that resonate with Western admissions officers

🏗️

Building something in your city A startup, community project, or innovation in Riyadh, Jeddah, Medina — Saudi entrepreneurship is compelling and uncommon in applicant pools.

🕌

Islamic identity with intellectual confidence Not apologetic, not performative — specific, grounded, and intellectually engaged. The prayer schedule that shaped your discipline. Ramadan that changed your relationship with discomfort.

📖

Arabic as intellectual passion Language, poetry, classical literature, identity. Arabic as the vehicle for ideas — not just a language you speak at home.

👨‍👩‍👧

Generational narrative First in your family to study abroad. Your grandmother's stories. What your parents sacrificed and what that means for your ambition.

🚀

Vision 2030 context You are coming of age in a country actively transforming. Being part of that — or watching it happen around you — is a compelling lens.

🤖

STEM in a Saudi context Robotics club in Medina. Coding competition in Riyadh. Science Olympiad. Regional firsts are powerful — "first in my city/school/family to..."

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Over-explaining Islamic concepts — Don't define what Ramadan is or explain the five pillars. Admissions officers can Google it. Write as if they already know.
  • Apologizing for your culture — Never write as if your background needs justification. Saudi identity is an asset, not a liability.
  • Performative Mecca/Medina references — Writing about the Haramain to impress or signal religiosity reads as hollow. If Mecca is in your story, it must be earned and specific.
  • Generic "my country is changing" essays — Every Saudi applicant can write about Vision 2030. Your story needs to be personal and specific, not a policy brief.
  • Translation-sounding prose — If you're thinking in Arabic and translating, get a native English reader to review your draft before submitting.

📋 Common App Structure

The Common App personal statement is 650 words maximum. Supplemental essays (Why School? Why Major? Additional info) are separate. The personal statement should reveal who you are — not list your achievements. Your activities section and transcript do that already. Use this space for something the rest of your application can't say.
